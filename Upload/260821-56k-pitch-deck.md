## 04. Paid Social [SOME] (56K analysis of the Meta account, 2024)

The current Vårdväskan Meta strategy overlooks two significant growth opportunities:

1. Optimize the campaign structure to adopt a full-funnel approach, increasing conversions & brand awareness while reducing cost.
2. Establish a Way of Working and a Creative strategy to improve creative quality and drive more traffic.

### Campaign structure

Full-funnel campaign structure model: Branding (broad campaign for brand awareness) → Prospecting (increase new purchasing customers) → Inspirational Retargeting (site/category visitors) → Feed-based Retargeting (product-level interest) → Activation / Cross Sell (activating CRM groups for loyalty and conversion rate).

**Current state (per 56K):** Vårdväskan's current campaign structure focuses mainly on prospecting, with no CRM activities. Approximate split: 80% prospecting, 5% / 15% on the retargeting layers. Spend in prospecting is high because proper remarketing is not done. Existing remarketing campaigns target all of Sweden — broad, lacking precision.

**56K recommendation:** refined structure of approximately 55% prospecting / 20% inspirational retargeting / 20% feed-based retargeting / 5% activation-cross-sell, plus branding. Open questions raised: LinkedIn for B2B? Build Beez with a separate funnel, like B2C?

With a full-funnel approach, Vårdväskan will: reach more people for lower spend; build brand awareness; get a larger retargeting pool; increase customers; get customers to buy again.

**Estimated impact (based on October 2024 account data):** CPM can decrease by ~10%; revenue will increase by ~8.4%; same ROAS and COS maintained. Estimated increased yearly revenue of 780 KSEK in SE from a simplified campaign structure.

### Creative strategy (56K review of current creatives, 2024)

- Attention spans are short; quick cuts and animations drive attention; the main message must be crystal clear within the first 2 seconds (Happy Socks look-alike example: colorful socks ad turned out to be a weight-loss company — message was not clear).
- Review of current Vårdväskan ads — "Will people understand your products in 1–2 seconds? — No":
  - The small font is very hard to see and read in social media.
  - A "se mer" (see more) button is used in the image (which cannot be clicked) while the CTA is "köp nu" (buy now) — confusing.
  - Clearly shoes are being sold, but without "skobidrag" (shoe allowance) users keep scrolling.
  - In the "3 för 2" ad the products can be misunderstood when taken out of the care perspective (could be read as school-related products).
- Other existing creatives are easier to understand but still with room for improvement: a GIF gives a quick, clear understanding of the product range; includes all persons working in the care industry; correct CTA used.
- 56K process for creatives: 01 creative workshop (review current creatives, evaluate competitor creatives, best practices, content plan); 02 content plan for paid social with a time schedule; 03 A/B-test creatives and copy regularly per target group; 04 evaluate creatives regularly in a creative forum with stakeholders.
- **Estimated impact:** working strategically with creatives typically increases CTR by 20%, giving Vårdväskan ~105,151 additional web visitors yearly without increased spend (calculated on the October 2024 account).
- Lift studies on Meta: brand lift study and conversion lift study to evaluate ad impact.

## 05. Paid Search [SEM] (56K analysis of Google Ads, Jan–Nov 2024)

- In a healthy ecommerce, shopping campaigns usually stand for ~80% of total revenue. Today 60% of Google Ads revenue comes from PMax and Shopping campaigns; 40% from Search campaigns.
- Potential in Shopping to drive more profitable traffic; great opportunity to add Demand Gen and YouTube to the mix.
- Reallocating budget to a 70/30 shopping/search split would give an estimated ~4.7 MSEK (50%) increase in revenue.
- Focus areas: Measurement, Shopping/Performance Max, Audiences, Search, Full Funnel.

### Shopping findings

- 1,240 of 12,407 SKUs stand for 91% of revenue through Google Shopping & Performance Max — only ~10% of products generate the revenue.
- 5,738 SKUs (45%) got no traffic at all Jan–Nov 2024.
- 82% of existing item IDs received less than 10 clicks per month; 46% received 0 clicks.
- **Zombie campaign** recommendation: flag zero-click SKUs with a script, move to a dedicated campaign to collect data, return to the main campaign when activated. Improves visibility for low-performing products, boosts account efficiency, recovers lost revenue potential.
- Bid analysis (top SKUs, e.g. Beez Stödstrumpor Clare, Littmann Classic III, name tags, Beez blood pressure cuff, Birkenstock Arizona etc.): 42% of shopping spend is allocated to products with bids too high; 11% of products have bids too low, indicating missed conversions. An improved campaign structure with spend reallocation would boost profitability and growth. (One example table sourced from Google Ads Skruvat.se November 2024 as illustration.)
- 75% of current item IDs have product titles under 50 characters, impairing shopping visibility and CTR. Recommendation: longer titles (~150 characters matched by Google; first ~70 visible). Template: Brand + Model + Type + Size + Color + Bullets + Material + Specification/use case. Example: "MBT Myto Sneaker Arbetssko - Damsko i Storlek 37, grå, lätta skor i Mesh-Design med Stötdämpning för bättre komfort och ergonomi" (142 chars) vs today "MBT Myto Sneaker Grey Grå. 37" (29 chars). Added search volumes: ergonomiska skor 590/m, skor med stötdämpning 880/m, arbetssko 390/m.
- Shopping setup must be tailored to business needs — segmentation options: category segmentation (Shoes & Sandals, Work Tools, Name Tags, Workwear); performance segmentation (bestsellers / mid performers / zombies-waste); product margin segmentation (<10%, 10–30%, 30–50%, 50–70%); AOV segmentation (high/mid/low); vs current segmentation (top products / catch all / low performance).
- Business-based segmentation questions: What products drive most growth and profitability? Strategic products/categories to win? Products with low sales run rate to activate? How different are GM1 and GM2 on SKU level and what to optimise against? What 1st/3rd-party data (e.g. competitor prices) is available?

### Audiences

- "With an audience strategy in place, we can allocate budget towards users more likely to convert — increased revenue and decreased COS."
- Successful audience strategy combines 1st party (website visitor data by ecommerce-event engagement stage; customer lists segmented into best/worst; bid modifiers / conversion value rules; RFM segmentation — Recency, Frequency, Monetary — into Star, Loyal, Potential Loyal, Hold and improve, Lost, Bad customers; can be broken down per category; Voyado can do this grouping, or 56K scripts) and 3rd party (In-Market, Affinity, Life events, Custom intent from competitor sites e.g. medistore.se, industry press e.g. vårdfokus.se, searched terms e.g. stetoskop, arbetsskor).
- Current audience implementation across 18 campaigns: Customer match lists 1/18; website visitors 15/18; In-market 11/18; Affinity 14/18; Custom Intent 0/18. The current audience strategy is insufficient, not realizing the potential of CRM data. Example: In-market audience "Socks" is 115x more likely to convert based on account data.

### Search campaigns

- Quality Score breakdown shows room for improvement for both ads & landing pages (ad relevance, landing page experience; source: Google Ads Jan–Nov 2024).
- Search campaigns (non-brand) 2024 have too-high bids relative to a 14% platform COS target. Example campaigns: P - Search - Tools - Exact (cost 287,653; avg CPC 10.67 vs affordable 9.87; ROAS 4.9 vs target 5.3); Shoes - Exact (6.18 vs 5.23; 4.49); Stödstrumpor - Exact (8.62 vs 7.06; 4.09); DSA (6.47 vs 5.05; 4.14); Name tags - Exact (10.33 vs 8.44; 4.33); Shoes - Broad (4.92 vs 3.58; 3.86).
- Nearly half (46%) of the search budget is allocated to 270 ad groups with COS above 14%; their average COS is 25%, almost double the acceptable threshold.
- Ad group cannibalization on important search terms: "stetoskop" qualifies for several ad groups (five different ad groups with CPCs 11.71–15.71 kr), creating internal competition, diluted ad rank, inflated CPCs, misaligned messaging and reduced conversion rates. Only 61% of "stetoskop" cost is attributed correctly to exact match.
- Opportunity to increase brand awareness and sales with Demand Gen and YouTube with sharp audience targeting. B2B audience process: 01 research the audience (roles, pain points); 02 build audiences on search & browsing behaviour (e.g. "Får man jobba utan strumpor i vården?", "Får man åka hem i arbetskläder?"; industry press, unions, systems); 03 use upper funnel — Video Reach Campaigns on YouTube for awareness, Demand Gen for specific audiences and demand creation. Demand Gen can reuse Meta creatives, reducing production costs. Examples shown for Beez Arbetsskor Klassisk Matt Pride and Namnbricka Militärklämma across Search / Demand Gen / YouTube.

## 06. Organic Search [SEO] (56K analysis, December 2024)

- Overall organic development looks good — Vårdväskan (SE) has been growing in recent years. Other markets do not show the same development — **DK has experienced a major decline**.
- Vårdväskan currently captures 43% of all clicks (organic performance), but there is significant potential within most categories (target CTR at rank 1–5).
- Vårdväskan is missing out on both awareness and commercial opportunities across the funnel. Example queries: Awareness ("Får arbetsgivare ta betalt för arbetskläder?", "Vad säger lagen om arbetskläder?", "Vem har rätt till arbetskläder?", "Får man jobba utan strumpor i vården?" etc.), Consideration ("Får man använda arbetskläder privat?", "Får man göra avdrag för arbetskläder?"), Conversion ("Vårdkläder online", "Bekväma arbetsskor vården", "Namnskyltar vårdbiträde/undersköterska/läkare", "Beez arbetsskor", "Beez sandaler"), Loyalty ("Vårdväskan", "Vårdväskan bussaronger").
- Focus areas: category expansion, B2B web, Digital PR & EEAT, tech hygiene.
- **Technical hygiene issues (all flagged ❌):** breadcrumbs in English (301s); broken (404) & redirected (3xx) URLs; metadata; canonicals; hreflang; pagination; JavaScript issues; image issues; security issues; faulty sitemap.xml; missing schema markup; internal links.
- **57% of all pages receive no clicks from organic search** (~700 pages, mainly product pages; source ahrefs.com). Enhance product pages with better descriptions and improved merchant-listing schema to generate clicks and improve CVR.
- AI-assisted product copy process: product data crawl → image analysis (Google Lens) → data consolidation → AI instructions → AI draft → validation (manual checks) → final copy. Tone tailored to brand voice.
- **Category expansion — "herr" (men):** Vårdväskan is currently not targeting "herr". Current: 200,000 impressions, 1,100 clicks, 0.5% CTR, position 31. Potential: 20,000 clicks, 10% CTR, position 1–5. Rationale: untapped market of male healthcare professionals, broadened customer base, new revenue streams, competitive edge as pioneer.
- **Namnskyltar (name tags):** current 35,000 impressions, 395 clicks, 1% CTR, position 8 → potential 5,200 clicks, 15% CTR, position 1–3 (GSC, last 12 months).
- **Brand + category:** current 10,300 impressions, 660 clicks, 6.4% CTR, position 3.6 → potential 1,600 clicks, 15% CTR, position 1–3.
- **B2B web:** the B2B website is currently noindexed, so Google cannot access it. 56K advises opening up parts targeting specific audiences: by profession (e.g. veterinär), by material (e.g. stretch, bomull), by colors, other (e.g. ekologiska). Competitor example: www.7days.se.
- **Digital PR:** in contrast to traditional PR agencies, 56K creates newsworthy, data-driven stories that earn links; can get coverage in major newspapers. Case "#2 Regions with the highest annual divorce rate": 20 years of SCB data broken down per municipality; national + region-specific press releases; results: 34 placements, 12 backlinks, 22 mentions, avg Domain Rating 56.

- In-house SEO team support: operational (keyword research, titles/metas, content briefs, content plans, redirect mapping), technical (audits, requirements, migrations), strategic advisory (goals & KPIs, roadmap, ways of working, organisational maturity, content strategies), digital PR & link building.

## 07. A holistic approach

- One team: initial and ongoing analysis to discover gaps and potential between SEM and SEO; dashboards for channel synergies and prioritisation.
- Synergies between SEM, SEO & SOME: 01 dynamic ad formats (PMax uses website data — synchronized SEO/SEM strategies needed to secure landing pages reflecting search behaviour); 02 landing pages to decrease COS & increase revenue (content gap analysis; more relevant landing pages increase QS, decrease CPCs, raise CR); 03 traffic split & prioritisation between paid and organic on brand and product level (use Google Ads search terms + GSC to find gaps; aim for both organic and paid listings); 04 creative/copy transfer between channels (scale Meta creatives to Google and vice versa; improve titles/meta descriptions from high-performing ad copy; A/B test titles and meta descriptions).

## 10. Appendix

**10.1 Content production:** scalable centralised process for website content across markets — 01 central content plan (master keyword research; master page copy and SEO elements: titles, meta descriptions, body copy, headings, internal linking; brand guidelines & tone of voice); 02 central copy production (Jasper.ai with keyword research, product supply, brand voice; client review); 03 feedback/iteration; client sign-off; 04 local copy production (Jasper AI translation, first batch manually checked, master sheet); 05 implementation in CMS by SP/56K, possibly bulk upload.

**10.2 Marketing Mix Modelling:** maturity ladder — Last Click (last-click measurement, UTM tagging, reactive) → Data-Driven Attribution (MTA, ad-hoc experiments, some predictive elements, combining web analytics/media/business data) → Marketing Mix Modelling (MMM + MTA for long- and short-term effects; structured incrementality experimentation; predictive budgeting and revenue forecasting). MMM outputs: marketing efficiency (spend and effect per channel; ROAS = incremental effect / investment), budget optimization (optimal media mix accounting for incremental effect, diminishing returns/response curves, adstock), total effect over time (interval comparisons, trend analysis). Closing note: the effect of brand investments has to be evaluated on a longer time horizon.
